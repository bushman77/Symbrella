alanysis
This one is cleaner again.

The front half of the sentence is now resolving in a much more linguistically sane way: what|pronoun|0, do|auxiliary|0, of|preposition|0, in|preposition|0, and the|determiner|0 all look like the right kind of winners for this context. That is a real step up from the earlier runs where you had things like about|verb|0 and in|verb|0.

The main bottleneck is still candidate supply. You still have missing_candidates: 17, but because this sentence is shorter you only kept 10 tokens, so the missing rate is actually worse here at 0.62963. In other words, the lattice is a bit smaller, but it is still underfilled. Most of the missing spans are MWEs like what do you, do you think, of events, events in, in the world, and the world.

What I like most in this snapshot is that the audit now looks more believable. weak_decisions: 6 matches the margins much better than the previous run did. Here, the weak ones are exactly the kinds of winners you would expect to be weak: you at 0.055666, think at 0.010886, events at 0.010888, the at 0.0, the fallback phrase at 0.0, and world at 0.010892. That is much more coherent than the earlier snapshot where weak_decisions: 13 appeared to count even obviously strong winners as weak. The new low_confidence_decisions: 9 metric also tells a clearer story: most winners are still barely ahead, even when they are the correct POS class.

The remaining semantic problem is now very concentrated: content nouns are still losing to verb senses by a hair. You still get events|verb|0 over events|noun|0 and world|verb|0 over world|noun|14, both by margins around 0.0109. That is not random noise anymore. It is a pattern. And it points to a specific missing ingredient in the scorer: local syntactic context is not strong enough to exploit the fact that of strongly predicts a noun phrase after it, and the strongly predicts a noun after it.

Fallback behavior also improved. This run has only fallback_winners: 1, for world today?|phrase|fallback, compared with the previous run’s fallback_winners: 4. So fallback is no longer dominating the back half of the sentence. That is progress.

So my read now is:

guardrails are healthy,
audit semantics are getting saner,
function-word disambiguation is mostly working,
MWE coverage is still thin,
and the last stubborn failure is noun-vs-verb discrimination for content words in local syntactic context.

This snapshot is the clearest argument yet for adding a neighbor-conditioned bias term to Stage1. Right now your weights are lex_fit, rel_prior, activation, and intent_bias. What seems missing is something like a context/POS compatibility feature so that once of resolves as a preposition and the resolves as a determiner, they can push the next token toward noun-like senses instead of letting noun and verb stay nearly tied. That is an inference, but it is strongly supported by the exact winners and margins in this run.

This is honestly a better place to be. The problem is narrowing. It no longer looks like “LIFG is confused about everything.” It looks like “LIFG now understands the scaffolding of the sentence, but still needs stronger local context to lock content words into the right syntactic-semantic lane.”
